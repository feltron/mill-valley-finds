    High-resolution elevation

    1. Download all four quarters of the circle from USGS: Northwest, Northeast, Southwest, Southeast. Each is a compressed .tif of about 10–20 MB at 2.5 m per sample, from the best data USGS has here (lidar where available). USGS can take up to a minute to build each one.
    2. Drop all four here at once (or one at a time; each adds its quarter).
    Drop GeoTIFF elevation files, or tap to choose

    1-meter lidar tiles from the National Map Downloader work too. Drop every tile that touches the circle at once; they’re combined and resampled to the 2.5 m grid.

    Sharper imagery · about 1 m

    For detail that matches the 5 m terrain, add nine 1 m tiles on top of the image above. Open each tile’s link, save the image, and drop it on the same square. Each one sharpens its part of the map as soon as it’s added.

    Streets & trails · OpenStreetMap

    1. Open the OpenStreetMap download from any one of these servers (they hold the same data; if one won’t connect or says it’s busy, try the next): . It asks for every road, trail and railroad within 3 miles of City Hall, including abandoned rail grades. It can take a minute and ends in a long page of text.
    2. Save that page as a file (File → Save Page As, or ⌘S).
    3. Drop the file here.

    If none of them load, open the same search in Overpass Turbo, press Run, then choose Export → GeoJSON → Download. If every server refuses to connect, your network may block them; a VPN or work network is the usual cause.

    Lidar relief · about 1 m

    Shaded relief of the bare ground, made by USGS from lidar with trees and buildings removed. Old roadbeds, building pads, ditches and fence lines show up as faint lines and terraces, even under forest. Open each tile’s link, save the image, and drop it on the same square.
